## 3. Corrections to the programme's standing beliefs

### 3.1 The "catalogued but ungranted" claim is false — **confirmed by execution, and refined**

Lane P2-B3's correction holds: **zero of the 147 catalogued capabilities are
inert-and-ungranted.** I can now give the mechanism and the line.

`functions/src/access/compatibilityRoles.ts:235-240` constructs the administrator
compatibility role as its curated list **plus every remaining catalog id**:

```ts
const ADMIN_ALL_PERMISSIONS = [
  ...ADMIN_CURATED_PERMISSIONS,
  ...PERMISSION_CATALOG.map((permission) => permission.id).filter(
    (id) => !ADMIN_CURATED_PERMISSIONS.includes(id),
  ),
];
```

So any capability registered in the catalog is granted to `admin` — and to `owner` by
composition (`governedBusinessRoles.ts:83`) — the moment it exists. The code's own comment
at `:232-234` records this as a deliberate choice and anticipates that internal-control
exclusions would belong in an explicit list.

**Refinement that matters more than the correction:** 21 capabilities are held *only* that
way, with **no governed business role declaring them at all** — including both `coverage.*`
ids, all four `equipment.compatibility.*` ids, `financialPolicy.profile.configure`,
`report.definition.delete`, `finance.visibility.businessUnit` and `.company`, and both
`admin.dataImport.*` ids. "Granted" is true; "granted to a job someone actually does" is not.

**Methodology warning, and I made the mistake myself.** A grep-based grant audit reports 17
of those 21 as ungranted, because they appear nowhere as a literal string in either role
file. My first pass did exactly this, and so did one of my own research sweeps.
**Grant must be resolved, not grepped** (`P3B3-ADV-030`).

### 3.2 Five source comments are wrong — **verified by execution**

`functions/src/index.ts:396`, `:405`, `:492`, `:515` and `:519` each assert that a
capability is "registered INERT and granted to nobody". A sixth equivalent claim sits in
the D4 block above `firestore.rules:1714`: "the `equipment.*` capabilities … are all
`active: false`, so no principal can be granted them either."

All are false. Executed:

```
admin | inventory.catalog.alias.read | PROD=DENY/inactivePermission | SANDBOX=ALLOW/qualifyingGrant
admin | inventory.balance.read       | PROD=DENY/inactivePermission | SANDBOX=ALLOW/qualifyingGrant
admin | inventory.returns.intake     | PROD=DENY/inactivePermission | SANDBOX=ALLOW/qualifyingGrant
```

An `ALLOW` is unreachable unless a role *holds* the capability. These ids deny by the
`active:false` gate alone.

**This is not pedantry.** The two gates are lifted by entirely different acts. Someone
reading `:396` would conclude that flipping an activation flag changes nothing until a
grant is made. In fact activation alone immediately grants the capability to every
administrator and owner in the system (`P3B3-ADV-014`).

### 3.3 `inventory.catalog.manage` / `.activate` — **confirmed ACTIVE and live in production**

```
admin | inventory.catalog.manage  | PROD=ALLOW/qualifyingGrant
admin | inventory.catalog.activate| PROD=ALLOW/qualifyingGrant
```

Neither carries an `active` key at all (`permissionCatalog.ts:1071`, `:1077`; the predicate
at `:1695` treats an omitted flag as active), and both are granted explicitly to five
governed roles plus a dedicated catalog-administrator role.

### 3.4 Render transport and nonprod migrations — **confirmed by reading, not executed**

The brief's two live facts check out against source:

- `functions/src/eosApi/server.ts:166-171` creates one server routing `/operations/*` to
  `eosOpsHttp.ts` and everything else to `adminPolicyHttp.ts`. The first refuses any path
  but `/operations/inventory` (`:176`) with a closed operation list of exactly one entry,
  `resolveMyCapabilities` (`:76-77`). The second refuses any path but `/admin/policy`
  (`:130`) plus `GET /health` (`:122`).
- `functions/migrations/` holds 18 files. `eos_crm` is #15, `eos_commercial` #16,
  `eos_finance` #17, AR #18. A nonprod database at 7 of 18 therefore has none of them.

I did **not** re-measure the applied-migration count or the 256/0 bootstrapped-admin figure
— see §7 UNPROVEN.

## 4. Top 10 findings, ranked by business consequence

### 1. The business cannot bill a customer from EOS today, by any role, in production
`P3B3-ADV-037`, `P3B3-FIN-001`. `finance.invoice.issue` resolves `prodAllow = 0` across all
48 roles. So does every other `finance.*`, `salesOrder.*`, `opportunity.*` and `report.*`
capability. The commands are written, tested, deployed and fail-closed
(`functions/src/finance/invoiceCallables.ts:112`); the capability is `active:false`
(`permissionCatalog.ts:283`); production activates nothing
(`config/environments.json`, `taylor-parts-production` entry from `:257`, no
`capabilityActivationOverrides` key). 37 of 147 capabilities are live in production and none
of them is commercial.

### 2. Customer master data is governed by a legacy role string, not by the capability model
`P3B3-ADV-002`. `customer.record.update` is ACTIVE and resolves ALLOW for seven roles
including `salesperson`, `salesManager`, `officeManager` and `generalManager`. The write
path is a **client-direct Firestore write** (`field-ops-app-vite/src/domain/accounts.js:78`)
governed by `isAdminOrDispatcher()` (`firestore.rules:22-24`), which reads a single legacy
string at `users/{uid}.role` and admits exactly `"admin"` and `"dispatcher"`. **Five of the
seven capability holders are refused by the rules that actually decide.** The repository
maintains a CI-enforced corpus of the remaining legacy sites —
`functions/src/access/legacyAuthorizationSurface.ts:52-74`, 44 sites across 22 collections,
down from 47.

### 3. The order-to-cash chain has a step in the middle that belongs to no governed role
`P3B3-ADV-015`, `P3B3-SALES-063`. `salesOrder.fulfill` and `salesOrder.service` are held by
`owner`, `admin` and `dispatcher` — and by **no governed business role**. Executed:
`salesperson`, `generalManager` and `operationsManager` all resolve
`DENY/noQualifyingGrant` in the sandbox, where the activation gate is lifted. Six roles can
book an order; thirteen can read one; none of the business-named roles can advance it. The
only non-administrator principal who can is the dispatcher — who is *deliberately excluded*
from billing authority a few lines away in the same file (`P3B3-ADV-022`).

### 4. In the only environment where finance is activated, almost nobody can see a number
`P3B3-FIN-021`, `P3B3-ADV-013`. FIN-004 requires **both** `finance.read` (the fact-family
gate) and a `finance.visibility.*` reach. In `platform-sandbox` the override array activates
`finance.visibility.consolidated` and **not** `.self`, `.team`, `.businessUnit` or `.company`.
Executed: `salesperson | finance.read = ALLOW` and `salesperson | finance.visibility.self =
DENY/inactivePermission`, in **both** environments. A Salesperson and a Sales Manager can
see no financial fact anywhere; an Accounting Manager sees everything, consolidated. There
is no middle.

### 5. A Ventana order can be fulfilled from a Taylor warehouse and nothing records it
`P3B3-ADV-001`. Sales Orders carry `operatingCompanyId`
(`commercialCompanyScope.ts:4-8`, written at `salesOrderCommands.ts:287`). Warehouses carry
one (`config/ownership/operating-company-roots.sandbox.json`). `allocateSalesOrder.ts`
contains no reference to it — the eligible pool is `status==ACTIVE` warehouses and nothing
else (`:119`). The module states the position explicitly: "there is no enforcement, and none
may be added until the census gate passes" (`commercialCompanyScope.ts:26-29`). And if the
answer is "yes, and it creates an intercompany obligation", that obligation has nowhere to
live: FIN-BLOCK-004 forbids an intercompany record type until the Owner rules the treatment.

### 6. Seventeen capabilities are built, granted, and can run in no environment at all
`P3B3-MGMT-050`. Not merely unactivated in production — absent from **every**
`capabilityActivationOverrides` array in `config/environments.json`:
`workOrder.labor.record`, `workOrder.labor.correct`, four of the five `finance.visibility.*`
reaches, `coverage.write`, `coverage.read`, three report fields
(`customer.notes`, `customer.accountOwner`, `location.accessNotes`),
`admin.credentialReset.initiate`, all four `equipment.compatibility.*` and
`equipment.model.manage`. Labour cannot be recorded anywhere, so it cannot reach billing
(`P3B3-FIN-061`). Territory and coverage are modelled, stored, commanded and dead
(`P3B3-ADV-024`). Administrator password reset is built end to end and unreachable
(`P3B3-ADMIN-035`).

### 7. An invoice cannot be voided, and the system is built as if it could
`P3B3-FIN-004`, `P3B3-ADV-019`. The Postgres invoice table has `void_reason`, `voided_at`
and `voided_by` with an all-or-nothing CHECK (`1758931200000_invoice-authority.sql:180`,
`:203-207`). The AR projection understands a `VOID` position
(`financeReadProjection.ts:41`, `:47`) and reconciliation reads it (`:142`). **No write path
sets any of it** — the migration says so at `:94-99`. Meanwhile `issueInvoice` does not read
the governed Sales Order at all (the outstanding P1.8 item,
`p1-fulfillment-billing-spine-spec.md:44`), so it cannot observe a cancellation. An invoice
issued in error is permanent.

### 8. Three authorities decide "may this person do this", and the repository forbade a third
`P3B3-ADV-039`, `P3B3-ADV-029`, `P3B3-ADMIN-060`. (a) Firestore Rules over a legacy role
string — 44 sites, 22 collections. (b) The governed capability engine — 147 capabilities,
48 roles. (c) The Postgres object/field policy store behind `/admin/policy`, with its own
role catalog and its own 4-action authority model
(`administrationAuthority.ts:56-60`, `:41-45`). `legacyAuthorizationSurface.ts:16-21`
records an **Owner constraint** that a third authorization authority must not be introduced,
and names (a) and (b) as the two that exist. An administrator narrowing a role on the
Roles & Permissions screen is editing (c), and every sales, CRM and finance command obeys
(b). Nothing in the UI says so.

### 9. Cash that arrives in the shape real customers send it cannot be recorded
`P3B3-ADV-004`, `P3B3-FIN-016`, `P3B3-FIN-017`. `applyPayment` rejects over-application
outright — "unapplied balances are not yet supported" (`paymentCommands.ts:129-130`), and
`unappliedMinor` is always written as 0 (`:150`). So: an overpayment cannot be recorded; a
deposit taken before invoicing has nowhere to sit; and a franchise parent paying three
franchisees' invoices with one cheque has no representation at all, because EOS models no
customer hierarchy. There is also no accounts payable of any kind — no supplier bill, no
payable, no payment run (`P3B3-FIN-027`).

### 10. Customer edits produce no audit trail, and employee edits do
`P3B3-ADV-034`, `P3B3-CRM-028`. `updateEmployeeProfile` is a governed callable with a
record-change-history read beside it (`functions/src/index.ts:243-245`). Account edits —
including payment terms and tax status, the two governed commercial fields — are raw client
Firestore writes producing nothing. Both `firestore.rules:1329-1331` and
`AccountForm.jsx:32-36` name the trusted server-side writer as the interim plan. Bulk
contact import is worse still: a client `writeBatch` (`contactImport.js:20`, `:38`) with no
contract, no job record, no environment guard and no audit — beside a governed import
pipeline that has all four (`P3B3-ADV-012`).

## 5. The seams that produced the most BLOCKED and NOT_SUPPORTED

Ranked by how many activities each seam accounted for.

1. **Activation, not grant** (49 `CAPABILITY_INACTIVE` citations). The single largest cause
   by a wide margin. `109 of 147` capabilities are `active:false`, and the gate fires
   *ahead of and regardless of* any role grant
   (`resolveEffectivePermission.ts:246-266`). The repository states GRANT != ACTIVATION in
   at least five separate comment blocks; the library's evidence is that the statement is
   true and its consequences are not yet widely understood.
2. **Owner decisions that were deliberately deferred** (15 citations). FIN-BLOCK-001
   (scoped financial visibility), FIN-BLOCK-002 (service billing), FIN-BLOCK-003 (cost
   authority — margin is *structurally* unknown and must render as unknown, never 0%),
   FIN-BLOCK-004 (intercompany — no record type may exist until ruled), FIN-BLOCK-005
   (no financial screen without an approved design source), FIN-007 §2 (approval
   thresholds unset), decision #145 (authority of record for financial figures).
   These are not gaps; they are recorded refusals to guess.
3. **The Postgres data plane has no door** (8 `SCHEMA_ABSENT_NONPROD` + 2
   `TRANSPORT_UNREACHABLE`). Sixteen authority modules exist under `functions/src/eosOps/`
   and `functions/src/eosCommercial/`. The transport in front of them exposes `/health`,
   `POST /admin/policy` and `POST /operations/inventory` — the last with a closed operation
   list of exactly one non-mutating read. Three of the four schemas the commercial story
   depends on land in migrations 15-18 and nonprod stops at 7.
4. **Deliberate client closure with no server alternative** (5 `RULES_DENY`). Mostly right —
   opportunities, sales orders, agreements, invoices, payments and report definitions all
   deny every client read and write for every principal including admin, with recorded
   reasons. One is an absence rather than a closure: an equipment record's `accountId` and
   `locationId` cannot be changed by Rules (`firestore.rules:1512-1521`) **and no command
   exists to change them** (`P3B3-ADV-010`).
5. **Objects the business talks about that EOS does not model.** No Quote object (the
   Sales Agreement is the quoting instrument). No price list, no discount schedule, no
   approval limit. No commission or sales-credit model. No customer hierarchy. No sales
   templates (2,228 lines of wireframe, zero implementation). No accounts payable. No
   statements or dunning. Line of business is an Account enum that the code explicitly
   forbids deriving anything from.
