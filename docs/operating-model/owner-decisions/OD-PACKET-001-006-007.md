## 1. THE DEPENDENCY ARGUMENT — why this order, and why not another

**The order is `OD-1` → `OD-6` → `OD-7`.** It is not a priority ranking. Each step is a **hard
prerequisite** of the next, and in each case the failure of ruling out of order is *specific and
nameable*, not merely untidy.

| Link | Why `OD-1` must precede it | What happens if it is ruled first instead |
|---|---|---|
| **`OD-1` → `OD-6`** | `OD-6` asks whether the **person axis** must be able to report an orphan. *Which* person fact is being checked depends on whether a second person fact exists. Under `OD-1` option (a) there are two person facts to check (record owner **and** accountable person) and they have different correct answers — historical ownership legitimately survives departure (ruling R-20's pattern), accountability must not. Under options (c) or (d) there is one | Ruling `OD-6` first hard-codes the check against **record owner only** — the one fact that, per Q11, *correctly* stays put on departure. The measurement would then be built on the axis that must **not** move, and rebuilt the moment `OD-1` answers (a) |
| **`OD-6` → `OD-7`** | `OD-7` asks where family-level handoff refusals must run. Test **H1** (*a handoff target must be a live `ACTIVE` employee at the moment of assignment*) is a **person-axis referential check** — precisely what `OD-6` decides exists. A handoff validator built before `OD-6` cannot enforce H1, because the fact it would test is not derivable | Wiring handoff first produces a validator that enforces the **family** refusals and not the **target** refusal. `[EXECUTED]` Probe A already shows this exact shape: `buildOwnershipHandoff` accepts `USER:EMP-OLD → USER:EMP-NEW` — strings naming no employee — while refusing arbitrary COMPANY ids with `NEW_OWNER_INVALID`. **The handoff path is already half-validated in exactly the way `OD-6` would fix** |
| **`OD-1` → `OD-7`** (direct) | Whether a handoff moves *ownership*, *accountability*, or both is the substance of the handoff. `OWNERSHIP_HANDOFF` is the only vocabulary EOS has, and it names ownership | Handoff gets wired as an ownership mover. Q11's finding — *"the thing that must be released on departure is ACCOUNTABILITY, not RECORD OWNERSHIP"* — then has no carrier, and the handoff vocabulary becomes the wrong tool permanently rather than temporarily |

### Why not another order

| Candidate order | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| **`OD-7` first** (it is the only one with running code) | `[EXECUTED]` The live `auditEventWriter` already accepts an `OWNERSHIP_HANDOFF` for `invoice`, `payment`, `part`, `auditEvent`, `roleAssignment`, `transferOrder`, `"notAFamilyAtAll"`, and an `account` given a COMPANY owner. **Wiring first is the one action in this chain that can make production worse**, because `OWNERSHIP_HANDOFF` is a valid `AuditAction` and the writer has **50 importers** in `functions/src` at baseline (re-derived here). This is the strongest argument for `OD-7` being **last**, not first |
| **`OD-6` first** (it is the cheapest — *"a measurement change, not a backfill"*) | It is the cheapest, and cheapness is why it is tempting. But see the `OD-1` → `OD-6` row: the measurement would be built against record owner, the axis that *correctly* does not move |
| **`OD-1` and `OD-6` in parallel** | Defensible, and the only genuinely arguable variant. `OD-6`'s **NO** branch (uphold ruling O-1) is independent of `OD-1`. Its **YES** branch is not. So parallel work is safe only until `OD-6` answers YES, at which point it stalls on `OD-1` anyway |

Occupancy and vocabulary are two independent prerequisites (EMP-EXPERIENCE §6): grant every Role tomorrow and a role-shaped queue becomes **non-empty and keyed on the wrong thing**. And for ownership specifically occupancy does not bite at all, because **no work queue anywhere is keyed on an ownership fact** (OWN-E2E §7 stage 5) |
