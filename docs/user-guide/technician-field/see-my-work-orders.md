# See my assigned work orders

**What this lets you do:** See the work orders assigned to you, grouped by what needs your attention, plus a snapshot of your recent performance.

**Who can do it:** Technician. This is your "My Dashboard" home screen. Admins and dispatchers do not see this view.

## Before you start

- Sign in with your technician account.
- Your account must be linked to a technician record. If it isn't, the screen shows "Your account isn't linked to a technician record yet. Contact an admin to get this set up." Ask an admin to link you.

## Steps

1. Open **Dashboard > My Dashboard** from the navigation. You land on a page headed "Hi, [your name]" with your current status badge and a count of **My Active Work Orders**.
2. Look at the four sections below your name. Each one groups your work orders by stage:
   - **Ready to Start** — dispatched to you and waiting for you to accept.
   - **In Progress** — work you have already started.
   - **Waiting** — accepted, en route, or arrived (started but not yet worked).
   - **Completed Today** — work orders you finished today.
3. Each card shows the work order number, priority, type, the customer reference, and any parts planned for the job. The count next to each section title tells you how many are in it.
4. Select a card to open its detail view. From there you can move the work order forward and record parts and notes (see the related guides).
5. Select **Back to list** in the detail view to return to your sections.

## Tips and common problems

- **A section says it's empty.** That just means nothing is at that stage right now — for example "No Work Orders waiting on you to accept." It is not an error.
- **Nothing shows up at all.** Work orders only appear here once a dispatcher has dispatched one to you. Anything not yet dispatched won't be here.
- **The list is live.** New assignments and status changes appear on their own — you don't need to refresh.
- **"My Performance Snapshot"** shows your all-time completed work orders, all-time parts used, and average job duration. These are lifetime totals, not this-week figures.
