As of 2026-07-31 the Owner directed that the platform serve **two operating companies under common ownership**:

- **Taylor** — the Taylor equipment dealership (legal seller on documents: "Taylor Freezer Sales of Arizona", 2825 E Chambers St, Phoenix AZ 85040, Dist. Acct No. 973202). Owns the full service/parts/support system being built.
- **Ventana** — a separate company, **national accounts** business. It **consumes the Taylor support system** rather than having its own.

**Why:** this is business-ownership context that exists nowhere in the code or git history. It changes the tenancy model of every future sales/billing entity.

Owner-stated rules:
1. **Branded templates** — every sales/service document has a Taylor variant and a Ventana variant, selected by operating company, not chosen at print time.
2. **Sales attribute by ORIGINATION** — a sale is Ventana's if it was generated from Ventana (not by customer, not by fulfiller).
3. **Service is an intercompany cost** — Taylor performs, Taylor bills Ventana. Revenue on Taylor's books, cost on Ventana's. One Work Order, TWO invoices (Taylor→Ventana at transfer price; Ventana→customer at customer price).
4. **Taylor also has its own National Accounts team** — the Retail vs National Account flag sits on the **Sales Order**, and exists in BOTH companies.
5. **Ice machine equipment is Ventana-owned inventory**; when Taylor makes a retail sale of one, **Taylor buys the unit from Ventana**. So intercompany is **BIDIRECTIONAL**: service flows Taylor→Ventana, equipment flows Ventana→Taylor.
6. **ALL parts are Taylor's regardless of brand** — parts inventory carries no company-ownership dimension at all, and parts are always **SOLD by Taylor's parts department** on every transaction, including to Ventana's own customers. (Consequence: the Taylor→Ventana service charge necessarily covers labor AND materials, since Ventana owns no parts.)
7. **Ventana also sells equipment externally** — to third-party service companies and to specially designated customers treated as national accounts. So one buyer can be a Ventana equipment customer AND a Taylor parts customer simultaneously; an Account's revenue is never single-company.
8. **Company Controllers can override ownership** — product-line ownership is a DEFAULT, not an invariant. Effective ownership = override ?? default, never re-derived once overridden. **The Controller role is DEFERRED to the Financials initiative** (Owner, 2026-07-31); only the storage shape is honored now — store ownership as a real field with a default, never recompute it from the catalog on read.
9. **Accounts declare `lineOfBusiness[]`** — TAYLOR / VENTANA / both, for reporting and salesperson assignment. Reuses the existing `relationshipTypes` array precedent on `accounts`: optional, additive, informational-only, no Rules change, no migration. Lowest-cost item in the whole initiative and independently buildable first.
10. **Salespeople are typically assigned to ONE company** (Taylor or Ventana), so company affiliation lives on the Employee and an Account with both relationships carries one rep per line of business.
11. **Signature capture is a three-rung ladder**: (1) ideal — on-device e-signature on the technician's phone for delivery and service; (2) fallback — print the *completed* form in the field, wet signature, photograph it into the app; (3) outside process — blank printable form filled by hand. Rungs 1 and 2 both need **file/blob storage, which this platform has never used** (no Firebase Storage, no storage.rules, no upload path) — the largest hidden cost in the templates work. A signature must be bound to an immutable rendering/hash of the document as signed, plus signer name/title/timestamp/capture rung.
12. **Templates must be completable in the field OR the office**, plus a paper path outside the normal process. Signature capture is **in-field/in-person only** — no send-for-signature, no remote approvers, no third-party e-sign (deliberate scope exclusion).
13. **Equipment pricing is a three-tier ladder**: TRUE COST to Ventana → WHOLESALE PRICE → SALES PRICE to the customer. **Ventana is structurally a wholesaler: wholesale is its ONE outbound price to every buyer** (Taylor, third-party service companies, designated national accounts alike) — it never sells at retail. Tier 3 is the reseller's (Taylor's) price. **Wholesale is NOT stored — it is entered at time of sale** (no price master, no price book, no pricing engine; same posture as the manually-entered `supplierName` on `reorder_purchase_orders`), with a required **append-only price-change audit trail**. That audit trail *is* the internal control: a hand-entered wholesale price with no reference value is the one number that shifts margin between two companies' books. Note the platform today has append-only *records* but **no field-level change history on any document** — this is a new capability.
14. **National accounts are a scoped exception to "no price master"** — either a set % discount field on the Account (recommended first: one field) or per-account price lists (a subsystem: item×account rows, effective dating, maintenance UI — defer). Key dependency: **a % discount requires a STORED BASE PRICE to compute against**, so a per-model list/sales price must be stored even though wholesale is not. The paper Sales Order already has a `DISC %` column.
15. **True cost is a per-SERIALIZED-UNIT acquisition fact that varies over time** (same model can cost differently week to week from the factory), immutable once recorded. Because equipment is serialized, costing is **specific identification** — which avoids the FIFO/LIFO question entirely for equipment (parts, being fungible, would still need a costing method if parts margin is ever required). Margin is always `sale − that unit's own recorded cost`, never a current or average value. Implies a **Factory→Ventana inbound purchase flow** where cost enters the system.
16. **Prices freeze at invoice**; changing them means cancel + create a new order — no amendment path. Reuses the existing Cancel/Void discipline (`reorder-request-cancellation.md`) rather than inventing a lifecycle.
17a. **Cost visibility is COMPANY-SCOPED data isolation, not a UI permission** — sales/management see cost, but **Taylor never sees Ventana's true cost, only its own cost from Ventana (wholesale)**. Neither company sees the other's margin. Needs a company dimension on identity (activates the reserved `employees.companyId`) and a **Rules-enforced** read boundary — client-side hiding is insufficient under this platform's own standing rule.
17b. **Parts need margin; recommended method is WEIGHTED AVERAGE COST** (one cost per part recomputed on each receipt) — it needs no ability to distinguish individual small items, which was the Owner's stated concern. Depends on an authoritative on-hand quantity, i.e. the `inventory_transactions` trusted-write path; cannot be built on the static demo catalog.
17c. **Part numbers change when the manufacturer changes for the same part** — needs supersession/cross-reference with ONE durable internal part identity and manufacturer numbers as attributes. **Directly conflicts with INV-CONVERGENCE's `partId == SKU` join key** — reconcile there, don't decide independently. Historical documents must keep the number actually used.
17d. Also required: **full factory PO + receiving** (where cost enters the system for both equipment and parts; watch the three-way naming collision with `purchase_orders` and `reorder_purchase_orders`), a **crediting/credit-memo function**, and **inventory depreciation/write-down** (largely Financials-deferred; per-unit cost + acquisition date already satisfy its data prerequisite).
18. **FIFO vs best-cost is an ALLOCATION recommendation, not a costing method** — a pure function ranking available serialized units, computed on read and never persisted, matching the `dispatchScoring.js`/`technicianRecommendationEngine.ts` idiom. Non-binding; deviations recorded. Forces two things: **serialized unit reservation** (or two orders sell the same machine — precedent is `fieldops_inventory`'s available/reserved split) and cost visibility control (a best-cost ranking *is* a cost disclosure). So buyer type is never a Ventana pricing input, and the intercompany transfer is an ordinary wholesale sale that happens to be between related companies — intercompany in its *books* treatment, not in its *pricing*. Ventana margin = wholesale − true cost; Taylor margin = sales − wholesale; combined = sales − true cost (what consolidates). True cost is likely a per-serialized-unit fact while sales price is a per-model catalog value — model them at different levels. The **service** transfer basis (Taylor→Ventana) is a separate, still-undefined number. Raises a capability the platform lacks: **field-level visibility within one document** (a technician completes work/parts/times/readings; pricing, rates, tax, totals stay office-only) — route-level ROLE_NAV_ACCESS cannot express this, and it must not be solved by widening technician route access.

**How to apply:** three separate fields, never derived from each other — `operatingCompanyId` (whose books; from origination; immutable), `performingCompanyId` (who did the work; `TAYLOR` on a Ventana job is the NORMAL case and is what triggers the intercompany charge), and `salesChannel` (`RETAIL` | `NATIONAL_ACCOUNT`, order-level). All four operatingCompany × salesChannel combinations occur; `NATIONAL_ACCOUNT` does **not** imply Ventana. Inventory **ownership is a dimension separate from location** — one shared warehouse holds Taylor parts, Taylor equipment, and Ventana ice machines side by side; today's `fieldops_inventory`/`warehouses` model has location only. Pricing must be modeled as a **rate context resolved by (billing relationship × what is being priced)** — service transfer, equipment transfer, and customer price are three different bases — not one price per line — otherwise it cannot carry both transfer and customer price and would need a full rebuild. Combined cross-company reporting must eliminate intercompany revenue or it double-counts every service job. This activates the Company entity from Future → Core and needs a Tier 2 ADR per [[feedback_delegation_charter_tier_model]].
