## Did 5 workers degrade quality or coordination — for this workload?

**No — for this review workload the opposite held.** Evidence:

- **Zero coordination cost:** all 5 completed first-pass, no retries, no caps, no failures. Scope separation was clean; the only overlaps (W4→W2 truck-stock, W2→W1 allocateSalesOrder) were *intended* handoffs, cross-referenced correctly rather than colliding.
- **Dedup discipline held:** workers self-flagged DUP #802/#818 accurately (W1-F2, W3-F5, W4 dispatch, W2 demo-surface) — the injected baseline worked.
- **Depth increased, not decreased:** parallelism let each worker go *deeper* than the #818 3-worker sweep — W1 converted grep-only guesses into proven defects; W4 turned one line into 7 correctness gaps; W3 turned one ENH into an ordered runbook. No shallow "locate-only" output, no contradictions, and each closed with an honest coverage caveat (W2 even flagged an unresolved substrate question rather than guessing).
- **Consolidation load** on the single consolidator rose vs. 3 workers but stayed manageable at dedup-only.

**Scope of this conclusion:** this reflects *this* review-and-discovery workload against *this* codebase's surface area, with tightly-scoped per-worker prompts and an injected dedup baseline. It is **not** asserted as a universal worker-count ceiling — the right degree of parallelism depends on the task's surface area, how separable the scopes are, and the consolidation budget, and should be chosen per run rather than fixed.
